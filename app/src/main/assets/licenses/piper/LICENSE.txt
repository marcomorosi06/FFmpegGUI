Piper voices (rhasspy/piper): the voice format and the published voices
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
FFmpeg GUI can dub with Piper voices (VITS networks with their .onnx.json
settings) that the user downloads and imports. No Piper code and no voice
is in the APK: the app reads a voice's settings with its own Kotlin, writes
sherpa-onnx's token list and metadata from them, and runs the network with
sherpa-onnx (sherpa-onnx/LICENSE.txt), which reads words with eSpeak NG and
piper-phonemize (sherpa-onnx/espeak-ng/, sherpa-onnx/piper-phonemize/).

The list of voices the app offers is taken from Piper's voices.json
(rhasspy/piper-voices): names, languages, qualities and sizes only.

Each voice has its own licence and its own recordings, stated in its
MODEL_CARD (shown in the app, under the voice): read it before publishing
what is made with a voice. The app reads the card for its licence and, for
a voice not free for commercial use (or for research only, or whose licence
it cannot tell), says so before every dub: keeping to the licence is up to
whoever publishes the video. A voice the user trains is the user's.

Marking of synthetic speech
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every dub carries a comment saying that its voice was made by a machine and
which one (in the WAV file the app makes), and every file exported with a
dub in it carries the tags AI_GENERATED and DIGITAL_SOURCE_TYPE (IPTC
"compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia"), as Article 50(2) of the EU AI Act
asks of synthetic audio.
